How BB84 works
1. Encode. Alice picks a random bit and a random basis (rectilinear + or diagonal ×) and sends one photon per bit, polarized accordingly.
2. Measure. Bob picks a random basis for each incoming photon and measures it. If his basis matches Alice's, he gets her bit exactly. If not, the result is random.
3. Sift (discard the mismatches). Alice and Bob publicly compare which basis they used for each photon — never the bits. Wherever their bases disagree, Bob's reading was a random 50/50 guess with no real information in it, so that position is thrown away entirely. Only photons where both happened to pick the same basis are kept; on average that's about half of them.
4. Check. They sacrifice a random sample of sifted bits to compare openly. Any mismatch reveals errors — a natural fingerprint of eavesdropping.
5. Why Eve gets caught. Eve doesn't know Alice's basis either. Measuring in the wrong basis collapses the photon's state, so when she resends it, she introduces errors roughly 25% of the time on bits she guessed wrong — pushing the error rate above the ~11% threshold Alice and Bob can tolerate.
6. Final key. The remaining, unrevealed sifted bits — proven low-error — become the shared secret key.
